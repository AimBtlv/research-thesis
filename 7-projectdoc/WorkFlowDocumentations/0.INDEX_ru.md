[^1]: What "Attested" Means

    Definition: An attested reading is taken from a real source rather than generated by the program.

    Explanation. The script checks each compound sign against sources in the following order of priority: OSL @v → Diri → OGSL → Syllabary_CM. The first reading found is recorded with the status ATTESTED_DIRECT.

    Breakdown of attested signs (Total: 1,604):     
        * OSL @v: 1,490   
        * Syllabary_CM: 69   
        * Diri: 40   
        * OGSL: 5 

    The remaining 776 signs fall into two groups:
    
     * `NO_ATTESTED_READING` (723): No reading exists in any source (e.g., `|(HI&HI).SUHUR|`). A compound that would be "nested" only by a match of letters belongs here as well (file P03).
     * `NESTED_IN_LONGER_FORM` (53): Has no reading of its own, but is part of a longer compound sign that does have one. "Part of" means: the same components in the same order (e.g. |DUG.SILA₃.BUR| inside DUG.SILA₃.BUR.NA); a match of letters only (A.BU inside SI.A.BUR₂) does not count (file P04).

    For 689 of these 776 signs, the `component_reading_inferred` column records a concatenation of the components' readings, such as |A.AB.BA| → 'u4-ab-ba. This is not an established fact, but a mechanical combination of each component's first reading. The example using 'u4 for A clearly demonstrates why this cannot be trusted. Under the project's rules, such readings are not counted as facts (confidence = inferred).
    
    Practical significance. I checked whether these 777 signs appear in the corpus:

    Corpus occurrence breakdown:
        * Attested:        484 distinct signs (81,254 tokens)
        * Without reading: 41 distinct signs (133 tokens)
        * Nested:          3 distinct signs (5 tokens)

    This means that 732 of the 776 signs do not occur in the Old Babylonian corpus at all. The most frequent ones are |DUR₂&DUR₂| (29), |GU&GU| (15), and |AMAR&AMAR| (15). This group has virtually no impact on the corpus comparison results: 138 tokens out of roughly 81,400 compound signs, or about 0.17 %. The catalogue is intentionally broader than the corpus, which is why it includes many of these rare signs.
