[^1]: What “Attested” Means

    Definition: An attested reading is taken from a real source rather than generated by the program.

    Explanation. The script checks each compound sign against sources in the following order of priority: OSL @v → Diri → OGSL → Syllabary_CM. The first reading found is recorded with the status ATTESTED_DIRECT.

    Breakdown of attested signs (Total: 1,612):     
        * OSL @v: 1,481   
        * Syllabary_CM: 69   
        * Diri: 43   
        * OGSL: 19 

    The remaining 777 signs fall into two groups:
    
     *`NO_ATTESTED_READING` (687): No reading exists in any source (e.g., `|(HI&HI).SUHUR|`).
     *`NESTED_IN_LONGER_FORM` (90): Has no reading of its own, but is part of a longer compound sign that does have one (e.g, `|A.BU|` inside `|SI.A.BUR₂|`).


   

    For 693 of these 777 signs, the `component_reading_inferred` column records a concatenation of the components' readings, such as |A.AB.BA| → 'u4-ab-ba. This is not an established fact, but a mechanical combination of each component's first reading. The example using 'u4 for A clearly demonstrates why this cannot be trusted. Under the project's rules, such readings are not counted as facts (confidence = inferred).
    Practical significance. I checked whether these 777 signs appear in the corpus:

    Corpus occurrence breakdown:
        *Attested:        492 distinct signs (92,516 tokens)
        *Without reading: 38 distinct signs (115 tokens)
        *Nested:          6 distinct signs (23 tokens)


    This means that 733 out of the 777 signs do not occur in the Old Babylonian corpus at all. The most frequent ones are |DUR₂&DUR₂| (29), |GU&GU| (15), and |AMAR&AMAR| (15). This group has virtually no impact on the corpus comparison results: 138 tokens out of roughly 92,600 compound signs, or about 0.15%. The catalog is intentionally broader than the corpus, which is why it includes many of these rare signs.
